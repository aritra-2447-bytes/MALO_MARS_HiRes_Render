CRISM (MRO) • OMEGA (Mars Express)
1. Data Selection
Instruments & Product Types: We integrate targeted hyperspectral observations from MRO CRISM (Compact Reconnaissance Imaging Spectrometer for Mars) and regional mapping scans from MEX OMEGA (Observatoire pour la Minéralogie, l'Eau, les Glaces et l'Activité). For local outcrop detection, we select CRISM FRT (Full Resolution Targeted, 18 m/px, 544 channels, 0.36–3.92 µm) and HRL (Half Resolution Long, 36 m/px) products in TRDR (Targeted Reduced Data Record, I/F reflectance) format. FRT/HRL products provide the spatial resolving power required to isolate stratified aqueous horizons (e.g., Jezero delta, Mawrth Vallis). For regional-to-global reconnaissance, we utilize CRISM MSP (Multispectral Survey, ~200 m/px) and OMEGA C-channel (1.0–2.7 µm, 300–4800 m/px) cubes, which provide continuous coverage across the Noachian southern highlands and Valles Marineris.
2. Preprocessing Steps
Photometric, Atmospheric & Instrumental Corrections: (1) Instrument Calibration: Spatial destriping is executed via column-dependent flat-field ratioing to remove detector column non-uniformity and spike artifacts; wavelength and optical thermal background drift are corrected using CDR calibration files. (2) Atmospheric Correction: Empirical Volcano-Scan Ratioing is performed by dividing observed spectra by scaled transmission spectra obtained over Olympus Mons (where atmospheric path length varies with elevation under uniform low dust opacity), normalizing the 2.0 µm gaseous CO2 doublet. For high-dust periods ($\tau > 0.8$), we couple this with DISORT-based (Discrete Ordinates Radiative Transfer) aerosol modeling incorporating aerosol optical depth ($\tau$) and Single Scattering Albedo (SSA) derived from the CRISM EPF (Emission Phase Function) sequences. (3) Photometric Normalization: Reflectance values ($I/F$) are normalized to standard geometry ($i=30^\circ$, $e=0^\circ$) using the Minnaert / Hapke photometric formulation to eliminate illumination artifacts across steep crater walls and chasmata scarps.
3. Spectral Analysis
Continuum Removal, Band Depth Formulations & Parameter Thresholds: Continuum removal is calculated over isolated NIR windows ($1.0–2.6\ \mu\text{m}$) using convex hull and linear endpoints. Normalized band depths ($BD_\lambda$) are evaluated via: BD_λ = 1 - [ R(λ) / (a • R(λ1) + b • R(λ2)) ] Specific summary parameter formulations (Viviano-Beck et al., 2014) and detection thresholds applied across the dataset include:
  • Clay Minerals (Phyllosilicates): BD1900 > 0.020 (molecular H2O in interlayer smectite sheet), D2300 > 0.015 (Fe/Mg-OH octahedral stretch in nontronite/saponite), and BD2210 > 0.015 (Al-OH stretch in kaolinite/montmorillonite). Composite Browse: CRISM PHY (R: D2300, G: BD2210, B: BD1900).
  • Sulfates: SINDEX2 > 0.020 (convex slope parameter between 2.10–2.40 µm indicating polyhydrated sulfates), BD2100 > 0.015 (monohydrated sulfate/kieserite OH doublet), BD2400 > 0.020, and BD1750 > 0.015 (gypsum doublet). Composite Browse: CRISM SUL (R: BD2100, G: SINDEX2, B: BD1900).
  • Ancient Water-Altered Rocks (Carbonates & Opaline Silica): MIN2295_2480 > 0.018 and BD2500 > 0.015 (CO3 asymmetric vibrational overtones in Mg/Fe carbonates), combined with BD2210 > 0.025 accompanied by asymmetric broad spectral shoulder (Si-OH stretching in amorphous hydrated opaline silica). Composite Browse: CRISM CR2 / HYS (R: MIN2295_2480, G: BD2500, B: BD1900).
4. Validation
Cross-Checking & Ground-Truth Verification: Detected spectral units are validated against reference laboratory standards from the USGS Digital Spectral Library and the RELAB (Brown University) database. Primary orbital benchmark control localities include Mawrth Vallis (continuous stratigraphy of Fe/Mg smectite overlain by Al-smectite and kaolinite), Nili Fossae (co-occurring olivine, Fe/Mg smectite, and Mg-carbonate), and Melas Chasma (layered polyhydrated sulfates). Direct in-situ surface ground-truth is confirmed using rover analytical instrumentation: (1) Jezero Crater (Mars 2020 Perseverance): PIXL, SuperCam, and SHERLOC spectroscopically confirmed the presence of Fe/Mg smectites in delta bottomsets, Mg-carbonates in the crater margin unit, and hydrated silica in delta topsets. (2) Gale Crater (MSL Curiosity): CheMin XRD confirmed saponite, nontronite, bassanite, and jarosite within the Glen Torridon and Mount Sharp sulfate unit. (3) Meridiani Planum (MER Opportunity): APXS and Mössbauer confirmed jarosite and Ca-sulfate veins in Eagle and Endurance craters.
5. Outputs & Astrobiological Relevance
Georeferenced Mineral Map & Astrobiological Habitability/Preservation Matrix: The finalized outputs comprise co-registered GIS rasters projected in Robinson projection (Mars 2000 Sphere) with band-depth confidence overlays. From an astrobiological standpoint: (1) Clay Minerals indicate ancient sustained, near-neutral to mildly alkaline aqueous environments (pH 6–8) with high water activity ($a_w \to 1.0$) during the Noachian Era. Their expandable 2:1 and 1:1 aluminosilicate sheets possess exceptional taphonomic preservation potential, entombing amino acids and polycyclic organics between mineral lattices away from solar UV and oxidative oxidants. (2) Sulfates mark the global climatic transition to arid, acidic, sulfur-rich evaporative regimes (pH 2–5) in the Hesperian Era. While hypersalinity and acidity constrain broad habitability, sulfates preserve microfossils through rapid evaporite crystalline entombment (halotolerant/acidophilic niches). (3) Carbonates & Opaline Silica represent Gold Standard biosignature preservation repositories; opaline silica precipitating from epithermal neutral-alkaline hot springs (e.g., Gusev Home Plate) entomb microstructures in microcrystalline silica (chertification).
Flagged Assumptions & Limitations
(1) Spatial Resolution & Sub-Pixel Mixing: CRISM (18–36 m/px) and OMEGA (300+ m/px) average surface reflectance over tens to hundreds of square meters; linear unmixing is required to detect small-scale veins. (2) Atmospheric Dust & Aerosol Obscuration: Martian atmospheric dust optical depth ($\tau > 1.0$) and nanophase ferric oxide dust mantles frequently attenuate bedrock absorptions; detections require dust cover index (DCI) masking. (3) Instrumental Cryocooler Aging: CRISM infrared cryocooler warming after 2010 degraded SNR at wavelengths $> 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$, creating elevated noise near the 2.4–2.5 µm sulfate/carbonate bands.
